The Foundation

The Holy Trinity of
Pick Strategy

Every single pick decision in survivor comes down to three factors. Miss any one of them and you're making decisions blind.

01

Win Odds

How likely is your team to actually win the game? The betting market — not your gut — is the most accurate signal available. A team with 80% win odds is a very different pick than a team at 60%, even if both feel "safe."

02

Pick Popularity

How many other contestants in your contest are picking the same team? A pick that 80% of the field makes barely helps you if it wins — and eliminates most of the field if it loses. Popular picks are low-upside, high-risk propositions.

03

Future Value

Are you spending a team you'll need desperately in Week 14? Every team you use is gone. The Chiefs available in Week 1 are the same Chiefs you might need when everything else is used up. Think 8 weeks ahead, not 8 days.

Core Principles

Play to Win.
Not to Survive.

The biggest mindset mistake in survivor is playing defensively. Here's what aggressive, winning play actually looks like.

Your Goal Is to Win, Not Just Last

Plenty of players make it deep by always taking the safe pick — only to run out of good teams in Week 12 with nothing left. Risk-aversion in the early weeks isn't safety. It's borrowing trouble. Play each week with the whole season in view, not just next Sunday.

Save Your Best Teams for When You Need Them

The Chiefs, Eagles, Bills, and 49ers will be available in Week 1 every year. They're also the teams everyone reaches for when they're desperate in November. Map the full season schedule before Week 1 — find the weeks when elite teams face their weakest opponents, and protect those slots from yourself.

Expected Value: What a Pick Is Actually Worth

Expected value combines win odds and pick popularity into one number: how much does winning this pick actually advance your chances of winning the contest? A 75%-win-odds team that only 15% of the field picks has huge EV. A 90%-win-odds team that 75% of the field picks has almost none. Always know the number.

Go Contrarian When the Field Crowds One Team

When 70%+ of your contest piles onto the same pick, a loss there wipes out most of your competition in one week. A slightly riskier contrarian pick — one with 65% win odds but only 12% popularity — can be worth far more to your long-run odds than the "obvious" choice. The field isn't your friend.

Contest Size Should Dictate Your Aggression

In a 15-person office contest, steady conservative picks might take it. In a 500-person contest, the math is ruthless — almost no one survives 18 weeks on safe picks alone, and you need to differentiate your path from the crowd early or you'll just be the last person to go out before the final. Bigger field = more aggression required.

Multiple Entries: Diversify Your Path

If your contest allows multiple entries, use them. Different entries let you hedge — one conservative path, one contrarian path. If the big chalk pick hits, your conservative entry survives. If it doesn't, your contrarian entry leapfrogs everyone. A portfolio of paths beats a single bet every time.

Stop Believing These

Survivor Myths
That Will Get You Eliminated

Survivor folklore is full of "rules" that feel smart but aren't supported by the data. Here's what to stop doing.

Myth: Never Pick Division Rivalry Games

The idea is that divisional opponents know each other too well, so upsets happen more often. The data doesn't support it. The betting market already prices in familiarity and rivalry dynamics. If the odds say a team is a 78% favorite in a division game, they're a 78% favorite — treat it like any other matchup.

Myth: Never Pick Road Teams

Home-field advantage is real — but it's already baked into the betting line. A road team listed as a 70% favorite has been adjusted for the fact that they're traveling. Road wins and home wins count the same in survivor. Trust the odds, not your instincts about where the game is being played.

Myth: Just Survive the First Month, Then Figure It Out

This one is actively harmful. The "just get through early" mentality leads players to burn their best teams on easy early-season games, leaving them exposed when it matters. Avoiding the crowd from Week 1 is often where the real edge is built — not in November when the field is already thin.

Myth: I Have to Use This Team This Week

You never "have to" use anyone. If a team is your last realistic option for a tough week but the value isn't there this week, wait. Forcing a pick because you feel obligated to use a team before a "bad" stretch often means using them at the wrong time. Value first, timing second — always.
